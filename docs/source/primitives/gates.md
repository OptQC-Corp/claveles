(sec:gates)=

# Gates & Operations

In this page, we enumerate the supported gates in both circuit and graph representations. The measurement
operations have a page of their own, {ref}`Measurements <sec:measurements>`.

{ref}`Introduction to Gate Operations <sec:ops>` introduces the same operations from the other end — what
each one does to a state, in pictures, with {math}`\hbar` written out, and what makes a gate set
universal — starting from what a mode is and needing nothing from the SDK. Read it first if
continuous-variable operations are new to you.

For every gate we give the symbol of the operation it applies, its generator (written in terms of the annihilation and creation operators {math}`\hat{a}, \hat{a}^{\dagger}`, with number operator {math}`\hat{n}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}`, or the quadratures {math}`\hat{x}, \hat{p}`), and, for the Gaussian operations, its Heisenberg action on {math}`(\hat{x}, \hat{p})^{T}`.
Each operation is a unitary generated by a Hermitian operator.

(sec:gates-conventions)=

````{note}
**Conventions on this page.**

* {math}`\hbar=1`, as everywhere in Claveles. Where {math}`\hbar` still appears in a formula below it is kept only so that the formula can be compared with the literature, and its value is 1.
* The ladder and quadrature operators are therefore related by {math}`\hat{a}=(\hat{x}+i\hat{p})/\sqrt{2}`.
* **A subscript on an operator is a mode index.** {math}`\hat{x}_1` and {math}`\hat{p}_2` are the quadratures of the first and second operand mode, and that is what a subscript says throughout this documentation. A gate whose name needs more than one letter therefore carries that name upright under a single wide hat — {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{CZ}}`, {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{CCZ}}`, {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{TP}}`, {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{TS}}`, {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{CPG}}` — rather than pushing part of the name into the place a mode index belongs. Three subscripts are labels rather than mode indices, and each is introduced where it is used: the {math}`\text{intr}` tag of {ref}`the symbol conventions <sec:gates-symbols>` below, the {math}`\text{th}` of {math}`\mathcal{L}_\text{th}` in {ref}`sec:gates-channels`, and the {math}`45` of {math}`\hat{S}_{45}`.
* {math}`S'` is a *parametrization* of the squeezing operator rather than a gate of its own; see {ref}`sec:gate-squeezing45`.
* A Heisenberg action is written as the matrix acting on the column of observables, that is as {math}`\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{O}\hat{U}` rather than {math}`\hat{U}\hat{O}\hat{U}^{\dagger}`. Where the column holds several observables, the operation acts on each of them:

```{math}
\hat{U}^{\dagger}\left(\begin{array}{c}
\hat{O}_1 \\
\hat{O}_2 \\
\vdots
\end{array}\right) \hat{U}
=
\left(\begin{array}{c}
\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{O}_1\hat{U} \\
\hat{U}^{\dagger}\hat{O}_2\hat{U} \\
\vdots
\end{array}\right)
```
````

(sec:gates-symbols)=

````{note}
**How the symbols name a gate.**

A `std` operation is what you write, so it carries the bare symbol: {py:class}`std.BeamSplitter <claveles.circuit.ops.std.BeamSplitter>` is {math}`\hat{B}`.

An `intrinsic` operation takes an {math}`\text{intr}` subscript **only where it is a different function of its arguments** than the `std` operation it corresponds to. There are six such pairs:

| Pair | `std` | `intrinsic` | The difference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Measurement | {math}`\hat{M}` (`MeasureHomodyne`) | {math}`\hat{M}_\text{intr}` (`Measurement`) | {math}`\phi` against {math}`\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} - \phi` |
| Displacement | {math}`\hat{D}` | {math}`\hat{D}_\text{intr}` | {math}`\alpha \in \mathbb{C}` against {math}`(x, p)` |
| Squeezing | {math}`\hat{S}` | {math}`\hat{S}_\text{intr}` | {math}`\hat{S}(r)` against {math}`\hat{R}(-\frac{\pi}{2})\hat{S'}(\cot\theta)` |
| Beam splitter | {math}`\hat{B}` | {math}`\hat{B}_\text{intr}` | {math}`(\theta, \phi)` against {math}`(\sqrt{R}, \theta_\text{rel})` |
| X-invariant shear | {math}`\hat{P}` (`PGate`) | {math}`\hat{P}_\text{intr}` (`ShearXInvariant`) | {math}`s` against {math}`\kappa = s/2` |
| P-invariant shear | {math}`\hat{Q}` (`QGate`) | {math}`\hat{Q}_\text{intr}` (`ShearPInvariant`) | {math}`t` against {math}`\eta = t/2` |

Everywhere else the two are the *same* operation, so both are written with the same bare symbol: {math}`\hat{R}`, {math}`\hat{S}_{45}`, {math}`\widehat{Arb}`, {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{CZ}}` and {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{TP}}` each mean one operation whichever module it came from.

The subscript exists to resolve an ambiguity, so it is dropped where there is none. The graph and machinery representations have a single gate set, whose parametrization is the intrinsic one, and {ref}`their operations <sec:gates-in-graph-repr>` are written bare. In those contexts {math}`\hat{B}` is the beam splitter of {math}`(\sqrt{R}, \theta_\text{rel})`, which in circuit-representation terms is {math}`\hat{B}_\text{intr}`, and {math}`\hat{D}` is the displacement of a phase-space vector, which is {math}`\hat{D}_\text{intr}`.

{py:func}`~claveles.circuit.visualize.make_figure` draws these same symbols, so a figure can be read against this page. The one box it draws that is not a gate is a state placed on a mode, written {math}`|\psi\rangle` — see {ref}`sec:circuit-drawing`.
````

The circuit representation offers two families of operations: the **standard ({py:mod}`~claveles.circuit.ops.std`) operations** and the **intrinsic ({py:mod}`~claveles.circuit.ops.intrinsic`) operations**.
The intrinsic operations are the low-level program elements that can be implemented natively on quad-rail lattice (QRL) CV-QC hardware, and the standard operations the machine can run are expressed internally in terms of them; the others (the non-Gaussian gates, two-mode squeezing, the channels and the partial trace) are not expressible that way and are applied by the simulator directly.
Every intrinsic operation other than {py:class}`intrinsic.Manual <claveles.circuit.ops.intrinsic.Manual>` is also covered by a standard one, so when building CV quantum circuits users can typically rely on the standard operations alone.
Accordingly, this page is organized so that the {ref}`standard operations <sec:gates-std>` come first, and the {ref}`intrinsic operations <sec:gates-intrinsic>` together with the {ref}`graph-representation operations <sec:gates-in-graph-repr>` are collected afterwards for reference.

(sec:gates-state-representations)=

The circuit-representation simulator can represent a quantum state in one of two ways: the *bosonic* representation, which expresses the state as a weighted sum of Gaussian components, and the *Fock* representation, which expresses it in the truncated Fock basis (see {ref}`Introduction to Quantum States <sec:states>`).
Only the Fock representation can hold the states produced by the non-Gaussian gates.
A gate is applied to a Fock-represented state by expanding it into intrinsic operations, so it can be applied as long as every intrinsic operation it expands to is supported.

Each circuit-representation gate below is annotated with a {{ bdg_hw }} badge when it can also run on the real machine, followed by a {{ bdg_bosonic }} or {{ bdg_fock }} badge for every state representation it can be applied to.
Hover over a badge to see what it means.
A gate is executable on the real machine when it can be converted to the graph and machinery representations, so a simulator-only gate carries no {{ bdg_hw }} badge.
The {ref}`Support Matrix <sec:support>` lists every gate with the same three answers in one table, and is generated from the table the SDK itself consults.

```{note}
Every intrinsic operation is supported for Fock-represented states, so any standard operation can be applied to them as well.
```

### Single mode gates

(sec:gate-displacement)=

#### {py:class}`std.Displacement <claveles.circuit.ops.std.Displacement>`

{{ bdg_hw }} {{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`\alpha \in \mathbb{C}`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\hat{D}(\alpha)`
```

The displacement operation {math}`\hat{D}` is defined by

```{math}
\hat{D}(\alpha)=\exp\left(\alpha\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{*}\hat{a}\right)
```

with Heisenberg action

```{math}
\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}
\overset{\hat{D}(\alpha)}{\longrightarrow}
\binom{\hat{x}+\sqrt{2\hbar}\,\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)}{\hat{p}+\sqrt{2\hbar}\,\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)}.
```

So {math}`\alpha` is a **complex amplitude**, and the shift it produces in phase space is
{math}`\boldsymbol{d} = \sqrt{2\hbar}\left(\mathrm{Re}(\alpha), \mathrm{Im}(\alpha)\right)`. Read the
other way round, a wanted shift {math}`\boldsymbol{d}=(d_x, d_p)` is asked for with

```{math}
\alpha=\frac{d_x+i d_p}{\sqrt{2\hbar}}.
```

This gate takes the amplitude; {py:class}`intrinsic.Displacement <claveles.circuit.ops.intrinsic.Displacement>`
takes the shift itself, as {math}`\hat{D}_\text{intr}(x, p)`, and is what this one is implemented with, at
{math}`x = \sqrt{2\hbar}\,\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)` and {math}`p = \sqrt{2\hbar}\,\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)`. See
{ref}`sec:gate-circuit-intrinsic-displacement`. Which of the two a symbol means is always visible from its
argument, so {math}`\hat{D}` is never written with a pair of quadrature components.

Either part of {math}`\alpha` may be feedforwarded from a measurement result, written as an ordinary complex expression over the measured variables:

```python
m0 = circuit.Q(0) | std.MeasureHomodyne(0.0)
m1 = circuit.Q(1) | std.MeasureHomodyne(pi / 2)
circuit.Q(2) | std.Displacement(m0 - 1j * m1)
```

Because {math}`\mathrm{Re}(\alpha)` and {math}`\mathrm{Im}(\alpha)` drive the two quadratures independently, each part may follow at most one measurement result, and only affine expressions (scaling by a real or imaginary scalar, plus a constant offset) can be written this way. A non-linear function of a measurement result needs an explicit {py:deco}`~claveles.feedforward.feedforward` function, which likewise reads a single result, as described in {ref}`sec:circuit-adding-feedforward`. A part that depends on two measurement results, such as their sum or product, cannot be feedforwarded.

#### {py:class}`std.PhaseRotation <claveles.circuit.ops.std.PhaseRotation>`

{{ bdg_hw }} {{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`\phi`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\hat{R}(\phi)`
*   - Note
    - Gaussian. A 1:1 alias of {py:class}`intrinsic.PhaseRotation <claveles.circuit.ops.intrinsic.PhaseRotation>`. Available in every representation.
```

The phase rotation operation {math}`\hat{R}` is defined by

```{math}
\hat{R}(\phi)=\exp\left(i\phi\,\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\right)=\exp\left(i\phi\,\hat{n}\right)
```

with Heisenberg action

```{math}
\hat{R}^{\dagger}(\phi)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}} \hat{R}(\phi)=\left(\begin{array}{cc}
\cos \phi & -\sin \phi \\
\sin \phi & \cos \phi
\end{array}\right)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}
=R(\phi)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}.
```

(sec:gate-squeezing)=

#### {py:class}`std.Squeezing <claveles.circuit.ops.std.Squeezing>`

{{ bdg_hw }} {{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`r`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\hat{S}(r)`
```

The squeezing operation {math}`\hat{S}` is defined by

```{math}
\hat{S}(r)=\exp\left[\frac{r}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{2}-\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}\right)\right]
```

with Heisenberg action

```{math}
\hat{S}^{\dagger}(r)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}} \hat{S}(r)=\left(\begin{array}{cc}
e^{-r} & 0 \\
0 & e^{r}
\end{array}\right)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}
=S(r)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}.
```

Implemented as {math}`R(0) S(r) R(0)` using {ref}`sec:gate-circuit-arbitrary`.

(sec:gate-squeezing45)=

#### {py:class}`std.Squeezing45 <claveles.circuit.ops.std.Squeezing45>`

{{ bdg_hw }} {{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`\theta`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\hat{S}_{45}(\theta) = \hat{R}\left(-\frac{\pi}{4}\right) \hat{S'}(\cot \theta) \hat{R}\left(\frac{\pi}{4}\right)`, a 1:1 alias of {py:class}`intrinsic.Squeezing45 <claveles.circuit.ops.intrinsic.Squeezing45>`.
*   - Note
    - Gaussian. Expands to the 45-degree squeezing gate and is available in every representation.
```

This gate is defined using the operator {math}`S'`, an alternative parametrization of squeezing with Heisenberg action

```{math}
\hat{S'}^{\dagger}(c)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}} \hat{S'}(c)=\left(\begin{array}{cc}
\frac{1}{c} & 0 \\
0 & c
\end{array}\right)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}
=S'(c)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}}.
```

It is related to the squeezing operator {math}`S` of {ref}`sec:gate-squeezing` by

```{math}
S'(c) =
\begin{cases}
    S(\ln{c}) & \text{if } c > 0, \\
    -S(\ln{(-c)}) & \text{if } c < 0.
\end{cases}
```

The phase rotation {math}`R` is defined in {ref}`sec:gate-rotation`.

(sec:gate-kerr-symbol)=

#### {py:class}`std.Kerr <claveles.circuit.ops.std.Kerr>`

{{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`\kappa`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\hat{K}(\kappa)`
*   - Note
    - Non-Gaussian. Circuit representation (simulator) only. Not available in graph or machinery representations.
```

The Kerr operation {math}`\hat{K}` is defined by

```{math}
\hat{K}(\kappa)=\exp\left[i\kappa\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\right)^{2}\right],
```

which is diagonal in the Fock basis with {math}`\langle m | \hat{K}(\kappa) | n \rangle = e^{i\kappa n^2}\delta_{m,n}`.
The Kerr gate is non-Gaussian: its generator is quartic in {math}`\hat{a}, \hat{a}^{\dagger}`, so it has no symplectic-matrix representation.

(sec:gate-cubic-phase-symbol)=

#### {py:class}`std.CubicPhase <claveles.circuit.ops.std.CubicPhase>`

{{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`\gamma`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{CPG}}(\gamma)`
*   - Note
    - Non-Gaussian. Circuit representation (simulator) only. Not available in graph or machinery representations.
```

The cubic phase operation {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{CPG}}` is defined by

```{math}
\widehat{\mathrm{CPG}}(\gamma)=\exp\left(i\gamma\hat{x}^{3}\right),
\qquad
\hat{x} = \frac{\hat{a} + \hat{a}^{\dagger}}{\sqrt{2}}.
```

Unlike the Kerr gate it is not diagonal in the Fock basis.
The cubic phase gate is non-Gaussian: its generator is cubic in {math}`\hat{x}`, so it has no symplectic-matrix representation.
Because the generator commutes with {math}`\hat{x}`, however, its Heisenberg action on the quadratures is still a well-defined (nonlinear) transformation:

```{math}
\widehat{\mathrm{CPG}}^{\dagger}(\gamma)\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}} \widehat{\mathrm{CPG}}(\gamma)=\binom{\hat{x}}{\hat{p}+3\gamma\hat{x}^{2}}.
```

### Two mode gates

(sec:gate-circuit-std-bs)=

#### {py:class}`std.BeamSplitter <claveles.circuit.ops.std.BeamSplitter>`

{{ bdg_hw }} {{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`\theta`, {math}`\phi`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\hat{R}_{12} \left(\pi-\theta-\phi, \frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) \widehat{Man}\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}, \theta, \theta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right) \hat{R}_{12} \left(\phi-\pi, -\frac{\pi}{2}\right)`
```

{math}`Man\left(\theta_a, \theta_b, \theta_c, \theta_d\right)` is implemented as {ref}`sec:gate-circuit-manual`.

{math}`R_{12}(\theta_1, \theta_2)` refers to the operation

```{math}
R_{12}(\theta_1, \theta_2)
=
\left(\begin{array}{cccc}
\cos\theta_1 &  & -\sin\theta_1 &  \\
 & \cos\theta_2 &  & -\sin\theta_2 \\
\sin\theta_1 &  & \cos\theta_1 &  \\
 & \sin\theta_2 &  & \cos\theta_2
\end{array}\right),
```

which applies {math}`R(\theta_1)` to mode 1 and {math}`R(\theta_2)` to mode 2; the phase rotation {math}`R` is defined in {ref}`sec:gate-rotation`.
The operation, when expressed directly as a matrix, becomes

```{math}
R_{12} \left(\pi-\theta-\phi, \frac{\pi}{2}-\theta\right) Man\left(0, \frac{\pi}{2}, \theta, \theta+\frac{\pi}{2}\right) R_{12} \left(\phi-\pi, -\frac{\pi}{2}\right)
=
\left(\begin{array}{cccc}
\cos \theta & -\sin \theta \cos \phi & 0 & -\sin \theta \sin \phi \\
\sin \theta \cos \phi & \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \sin \phi & 0 \\
0 & \sin \theta \sin \phi & \cos \theta & -\sin \theta \cos \phi \\
\sin \theta \sin \phi & 0 & \sin \theta \cos \phi & \cos \theta
\end{array}\right).
```

```{note}
This decomposition is what graph conversion produces, so it is what the machine runs. The simulator is
sent {py:class}`std.BeamSplitter <claveles.circuit.ops.std.BeamSplitter>` itself, undecomposed, because the
gate has a proto operation type of its own.
```

The same operation is generated by

```{math}
\hat{B}(\theta, \phi)=\exp\left[\theta\left(e^{i\phi}\hat{a}_1\hat{a}_2^{\dagger} - e^{-i\phi}\hat{a}_1^{\dagger}\hat{a}_2\right)\right],
```

which is the form {ref}`Introduction to Gate Operations <sec:ops-beam-splitter>` uses, and which makes
the conservation of {math}`\hat{n}_1+\hat{n}_2` immediate. Note that
{py:class}`intrinsic.BeamSplitter <claveles.circuit.ops.intrinsic.BeamSplitter>` in
{ref}`sec:gate-bs-symbol` is a *differently parametrized* beam splitter {math}`\hat{B}_\text{intr}`,
taking a reflectivity {math}`\sqrt{R}` and a relative phase {math}`\theta_\text{rel}` rather than
{math}`\theta` and {math}`\phi`; it is not the same function of its two arguments.

#### {py:class}`std.TwoModeShear <claveles.circuit.ops.std.TwoModeShear>`

{{ bdg_hw }} {{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}

    - Gaussian. Expands to the two-mode shear gate and is available in every representation.
```

The two-mode shear operation {math}`\widehat{\mathrm{TP}}` is defined by

```{math}
\widehat{\mathrm{TP}}(a, b)=\exp\left[i\left(a\hat{x}_1^{2}+a\hat{x}_2^{2}+b\hat{x}_1\hat{x}_2\right)\right]
```

with Heisenberg action

```{math}
\widehat{\mathrm{TP}}^{\dagger}(a, b)\left(\begin{array}{c}
\hat{x}_1 \\
\hat{x}_2 \\
\hat{p}_1 \\
\hat{p}_2
\end{array}\right) \widehat{\mathrm{TP}}(a, b)=
\left(\begin{array}{cccc}
1 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \\
2 a & b & 1 & 0 \\
b & 2 a & 0 & 1
\end{array}\right)
\left(\begin{array}{c}
\hat{x}_1 \\
\hat{x}_2 \\
\hat{p}_1 \\
\hat{p}_2
\end{array}\right)
=\mathrm{TP}(a, b)\left(\begin{array}{c}
\hat{x}_1 \\
\hat{x}_2 \\
\hat{p}_1 \\
\hat{p}_2
\end{array}\right).
```

\hat{T}^{\dagger}(g)\left(\begin{array}{c}

\hat{p}_2 \\
\hat{p}_3

\hat{x}_2 \\

\hat{p}_1 - g\,\hat{x}_2\hat{p}_3 \\
\hat{p}_2 - g\,\hat{x}_1\hat{p}_3 \\
\hat{p}_3
\end{array}\right).

(sec:gates-channels)=

### Channels

Unlike the gates above, the operations in this section are not unitaries but completely positive, trace-preserving (CPTP) *channels* that model loss and thermal noise, which is why they are written with a calligraphic {math}`\mathcal{L}` rather than a hatted operator. Both backends support them and represent the resulting mixed state directly: the bosonic backend through its covariance matrix, the Fock backend by keeping the environment mode in the state as a purification. Applying a channel therefore makes a saved Fock state come back as a density matrix rather than a state vector.

```{note}
On the Fock backend, holding the environment in the state costs extra degrees of freedom, which makes the bond dimension of the underlying tensor network grow faster than in an equivalent unitary circuit. Raising {py:attr}`~claveles.client.simulator_client.SimulatorClient.max_bond_dim` explicitly is recommended for circuits that use these channels; see {ref}`sec:simulation-fock-options`.
```

(sec:gate-loss-channel-symbol)=

#### {py:class}`std.LossChannel <claveles.circuit.ops.std.LossChannel>`

{{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`T`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\mathcal{L}(T)`: pure-loss channel with transmittance {math}`T \in [0, 1]`
*   - Note
    - CPTP channel (not a unitary). Circuit representation (simulator) only, on the bosonic and Fock backends. It is not available in graph or machinery representations.
```

The loss channel couples the mode (annihilation operator {math}`\hat{a}`) to an environment mode in the vacuum (annihilation operator {math}`\hat{b}`) via {math}`\hat{a} \to \sqrt{T}\,\hat{a} + \sqrt{1 - T}\,\hat{b}` and traces the environment out. On a Gaussian state it acts on the mean vector {math}`\boldsymbol{\mu}` and covariance matrix {math}`\boldsymbol{\Sigma}` of the mode as

```{math}
\boldsymbol{\mu} \to \sqrt{T}\,\boldsymbol{\mu},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\Sigma} \to T\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma} + (1 - T)\,\frac{\hbar}{2}\,\mathbf{I},
```

with any cross-correlations to other modes scaled by {math}`\sqrt{T}`. {math}`T = 1` is the identity and {math}`T = 0` maps the mode to the vacuum.

(sec:gate-thermal-loss-channel-symbol)=

#### {py:class}`std.ThermalLossChannel <claveles.circuit.ops.std.ThermalLossChannel>`

{{ bdg_bosonic }} {{ bdg_fock }}

```{list-table}
:stub-columns: 1

*   - Parameters
    - {math}`T`, {math}`\bar{n}`
*   - Operation
    - {math}`\mathcal{L}_\text{th}(T, \bar{n})`: thermal-loss channel with transmittance {math}`T \in [0, 1]` and environment mean photon number {math}`\bar{n} \geq 0`
*   - Note
    - CPTP channel (not a unitary). Circuit representation (simulator) only, on the bosonic and Fock backends. It is not available in graph or machinery representations.
```

The thermal-loss channel couples the mode (annihilation operator {math}`\hat{a}`) to an environment mode in a thermal state with mean photon number {math}`\bar{n}` (annihilation operator {math}`\hat{b}`) via {math}`\hat{a} \to \sqrt{T}\,\hat{a} + \sqrt{1 - T}\,\hat{b}` and traces the environment out. On a Gaussian state it acts as

```{math}
\boldsymbol{\mu} \to \sqrt{T}\,\boldsymbol{\mu},
\qquad
\boldsymbol{\Sigma} \to T\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma} + (1 - T)\,\frac{\hbar}{2}(2\bar{n} + 1)\,\mathbf{I},
```

with any cross-correlations to other modes scaled by {math}`\sqrt{T}`. The added term is the covariance of the {ref}`thermal equilibrium state <sec:states>` of the environment, so at {math}`T = 0` the mode is mapped to that thermal state. Setting {math}`\bar{n} = 0` recovers the {py:class}`std.LossChannel <claveles.circuit.ops.std.LossChannel>`.

(sec:gates-graph-through-and-swap)=

#### Through and swap

Each operation below is given by the four measurement angles {math}`(\theta_a, \theta_b, \theta_c, \theta_d)`
of the macronode it is placed on. A macronode {math}`k` takes in two modes, one from macronode {math}`k-1` and
one from macronode {math}`k-N`, where {math}`N` is the number of local macronodes. It sends two modes on, to
macronodes {math}`k+1` and {math}`k+N`. In the *through* case, the mode from {math}`k-1` leaves towards
{math}`k+1` and the mode from {math}`k-N` towards {math}`k+N`. In the *swap* case, the mode from
{math}`k-1` goes to {math}`k+N` and the mode from {math}`k-N` to {math}`k+1`. The `swap` argument of a
graph operation chooses between the two. A single-mode operation applies the same gate to both modes in
either case.

The measurement angles are shown only for the through case.
They are omitted for the swap case, as swapping the input modes within each macronode is achieved by exchanging the first two measurement angles: {math}`(\theta_b, \theta_a, \theta_c, \theta_d)` performs the operation of {math}`(\theta_a, \theta_b, \theta_c, \theta_d)` followed by a swap.
